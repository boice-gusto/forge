Acme / Forge engineering presentation

From intake to merge.

A controlled path from an ambiguous Slack message or Jira issue to a reviewable engineering outcome—without confusing a request with permission to act.

Use ← and →, the buttons below, or normal scrolling to move through eight slides.

01 / Normalize intake

Give incoming work one trustworthy shape.

A connector translates the raw message or issue into the Forge-owned WorkflowRequest contract.

What happens

Transport detail becomes a typed request.

Title, evidence, source, principal, classification, and an idempotency key are normalized before any workflow is chosen.

Why it matters

Raw payloads are unstable and easy to misread.

Slack and Jira do not need to leak through the system as special cases or unvalidated blobs.

Benefit

Every downstream control sees the same facts.

Teams get repeatable routing, better evidence, and less manual interpretation at the first handoff.

02 / Validate & authenticate

Accept work only at a trusted boundary.

The API validates the request, authenticates the caller, allowlists the workflow, and derives stable server-side identifiers.

What happens

Malformed or unapproved requests stop here.

Schema validation rejects bad shapes; authentication establishes who is acting; the workflow allowlist limits what can run.

Why it matters

A body field is not an identity claim.

Authorization context must come from the authenticated boundary, not from a caller saying they are an administrator or a Slack connector.

Benefit

Safe intake with stable retries.

Idempotency prevents duplicate starts while server-controlled identity and workflow versions shrink the attack surface.

03 / Policy decision

Decide the boundary before the action.

Runtime sends trusted actor, source, classification, workflow, and action hash to fail-closed policy.

What happens

Policy allows, denies, or asks for approval.

The decision is made against a small Forge-owned input contract rather than provider prompts or arbitrary connector data.

Why it matters

Not every request should have the same power.

A marketing brief and an external publication can carry different risk, even if they entered through the same route.

Benefit

Automation is constrained by intent and context.

Rules stay auditable and portable while policy evaluator errors default to denial instead of accidental permission.

04 / Human approval

Make the accountable decision explicit.

Sensitive work checkpoints with the exact run, action, argument hash, policy reason, and expiry bound to the decision.

What happens

Work pauses; a human approves or rejects.

The approval record is durable and can authorize only its matching action before it expires.

Why it matters

“Someone said yes” is not a control.

Approval must not drift to a different run, action, or changed set of inputs while work is waiting.

Benefit

Faster decisions with a defensible trail.

Operators see the action they are authorizing, and reviewers can later establish who decided what and why.

05 / Durable effect

Perform approved work once—even after disruption.

The approved action enters a durable outbox; a lease protects the dispatch while recovery distinguishes completed, retryable, and indeterminate work.

What happens

The effect is recorded before it is dispatched.

A stable operation key makes a retry safe and gives the effect adapter a bounded unit of work.

Why it matters

Networks and workers fail at inconvenient moments.

Without durable state, a restart can silently lose an effect or repeat it with no way to reconcile the result.

Benefit

Recovery without duplicate side effects.

The system can continue after interruption while preserving an operational record of what was attempted and completed.

06 / Review & merge

Hand engineers evidence, not an opaque automation result.

The workflow produces a reviewable brief, linked evidence, decision history, and a clear result for the normal repository review and merge process.

What happens

Humans review the proposed change in their normal tools.

Forge prepares structured work and traceability; repository checks and human review retain ownership of the merge decision.

Why it matters

Merge authority belongs to the engineering process.

Intake automation should accelerate understanding, not bypass code review, CI, or ownership boundaries.

Benefit

Less coordination cost, same engineering rigor.

Teams move from request to a well-framed, auditable review without replacing their established release controls.
