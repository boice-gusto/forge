Acme / Forge operating brief

From intake to merge.

Turn an ambiguous inbound request into a reviewable, policy-controlled, traceable change—without treating a chat message or issue as permission to act.

A local engineering briefing for the path between an incoming request and the normal repository review and merge process.

The control path

Six deliberate transitions

  1. Normalize intakeA Slack message or Jira issue becomes a typed WorkflowRequest.
  2. Validate & authenticateThe API validates the DTO, authenticates the boundary, and applies allowlisting plus idempotency.
  3. Policy decisionThe runtime passes trusted context to policy before sensitive work continues.
  4. Human approvalA checkpoint makes the accountable decision explicit and reviewable.
  5. Durable effectAn outbox dispatches the approved effect exactly once, with recovery built in.
  6. Review & mergeAn engineering brief and evidence enter the normal repository review and merge process.

Why these controls matter

Automation with an accountable trail

  • Fewer handoffsIncoming work gains a consistent shape instead of a bespoke interpretation at every boundary.
  • Safer automationAuthentication, allowlisting, and policy decisions set limits before work creates an effect.
  • Durable recoveryIdempotency, checkpoints, and the outbox make interrupted work recoverable without duplication.
  • Auditable approvalsHuman decisions attach to a specific run, evidence set, and resulting engineering brief.

Simulation boundary

These are deterministic simulations.

The examples are local, finite fixtures. They do not contact Slack, Jira, GitHub, or a production merge service; they require no network and no credentials. The executable acceptance test proves the routing and approval behavior.
